# TubeNavigator — User Manual (v1.12, 23 Nov 2026 — verified on the iPhone 18 Pro simulator)

A personal iPhone/iPad app for getting around the London Underground, built for the Dec 15–24, 2026 London trip. No account, no ads, no App Store — it runs from Xcode on your MacBook Pro.

## The five tabs

**Plan** — type in From and To and pick from the suggestions that appear as you type: your saved places and favourites come first (the hotel is top of the list on "wilde", "hotel" or "paddington"), then stations, pubs, or a postcode / address / place name. Then tap Plan journey. (A pub plans from the pub itself — the walk from the pub to its station is the first leg. A typed postcode or place works too; TfL works it out. If a name matches more than one place, the app shows TfL's "Did you mean…?" shortlist to pick from instead of an error — that is what a short hotel search used to trip over.)
- **The line under each field tells you what was actually found.** When you pick a suggestion, the field fills in and a line appears underneath it saying what it is — "✓ Bakerloo · Circle · District … — Zone 1" for a station, "✓ Your place · 336-337 Strand, London WC2R 1HH" for a saved place, "✓ Found place · 336-337 Strand, London, WC2R 1HA" for a place the phone looked up. If you have only typed and picked nothing, it says "Nothing picked yet — tap a suggestion below." in orange. If you picked the raw "Use '…'" row (typed text, never looked up), it says "“…” not looked up — TfL will guess the place." in orange. Typed text alone is never confirmed — that is the fix for the ME Hotel problem, where "me hotel, london" used to plan silently from the wrong hotel (the Radisson Blu in Seven Dials) with nothing to say so.
- **Look up a place on the phone.** If the suggestions do not include the place you want, tap "Look up '…'" under the suggestions. The phone searches for the place by name and offers what it found, with the address — tap it and the field fills in with the confirmation line. For example, typing "me hotel" and tapping Look up finds ME London, 336-337 Strand, and planning from there starts at Aldwych on the Strand (about 17 minutes to London Bridge by bus), not Seven Dials.
- Journeys come live from Transport for London (TfL), with total minutes, departure/arrival times, and each leg (line, direction, stations, walking bits).
- Tap a journey to see every leg in detail, with a map of the route and "Open in Apple Maps" / "Open in Google Maps" buttons that hand the same From and To to Maps for door-to-door routing. "Mind the gap." is at the bottom of the detail.
- Swap button flips From and To.
- Step-free route toggle asks TfL for a step-free-to-platform route.
- Your places and favourites: the Wilde Aparthotels London Paddington hotel is saved with its own coordinates (4 North Wharf Road, London W2 1NW), so it plans from the hotel door — walk to Edgware Road (Bakerloo), about 30 minutes to London Bridge — with no name guessing. (TfL's name lookup for the hotel used to resolve to the Covent Garden Wilde, and short wordings came back ambiguous; the saved place sidesteps both.) **ME London is saved the same way** — 336-337 Strand, London WC2R 1HH — for your friend's stay there; it plans from the Strand (walk to Aldwych) and records in Recent as Charing Cross → destination. Favourite stations are pre-loaded: Paddington, London Bridge (Borough Market), Westminster, Oxford Circus, King's Cross St. Pancras, Green Park, Bank, Victoria. Star any pub in the pub list and it joins the favourites too. Add your own places (a market, a friend's flat) from the Places tab — "Add a place" — by name and address; the app looks the address up once and saves the coordinates, and records the nearest station too, so the place shows up in Recent and can be saved as a route just like the hotel. (Dishoom High Street Kensington, 4 Derry St, London W8 5SE, was added before that and its nearest station — High Street Kensington — was filled in afterwards.)
- **Plan for a later time** — turn the toggle on in the When section, pick a date and time, and choose Leave at or Arrive by. TfL plans from the published timetable, so a strike or closure can still shift the route — cross-check on the Planned tab closer to the day. (Useful in the evening: at ~4 AM London time live planning only shows night buses, so a 10 AM plan for tomorrow shows the real Tube routing.)
- **Recent** — every journey you plan is remembered here automatically (the last 10, most recent first). Tap one to re-plan it live with current times. It is separate from Saved routes, which are the named ones you choose to keep.
- **Saved routes** — tap "Save this route" (on the Plan tab or on a journey detail) and give it a name, e.g. "Hotel → Borough Market". Saved routes appear in their own section on the Plan tab; tap one to load the stations (and its date/time, if it has one) and re-plan live. A saved route stores the station pair only — times are recalculated live, so it needs signal. Swipe a saved route to rename or delete it.
- **Pub crawl** — "Plan a pub crawl" on the Plan tab. The first-day route is built in (Borough Market → George Inn → Mayflower → Prospect of Whitby → The Grapes), plus all 327 pubs — 326 from your Historic Pubs catalogue, each matched to its nearest station, plus the Spaniards Inn. Search by pub, area, or station. Tap a pub to see why to go, its opening hours, its address, and how far its nearest station is — a few outer-London pubs are a long way from any Tube station, and the catalogue's own suggestion is shown there too — then tap "Plan journey to this pub" to plan the hop to the pub itself, walk included. Tap the star on any pub row (or "Add to favourites" in its detail) to favourite it; favourite pubs sit in Favourites on the Places tab and come first in Plan suggestions.
- Plan before you go underground — there is no signal down there. The station list itself works offline; live journeys and line status need a connection.

**Status** — live status for the Tube, DLR and Elizabeth line. Green is Good Service, orange is a delay or part-closure, grey is closed. Pull down to refresh. At the top is the trip countdown ("29 days to London" as of Nov 16, "Day N of the London trip" while you are there) and the weather strip: current conditions for where you are, today's high/low, wind, and the hourly forecast for the rest of today (Fahrenheit). It uses your phone's location and now shows the place name (e.g. Maineville at home, London on the trip, Paddington near the hotel); if location is off it falls back to Paddington. It needs a signal — underground it shows the last weather it fetched.

**Boards** (new in v1.10) — live departure boards for seven stations: Paddington, Canary Wharf, King's Cross St. Pancras, Liverpool Street, London Bridge, Victoria, Waterloo. Tap a station to open its board, then tap a line chip across the top to switch lines. Each platform shows the next 5 trains: where it's going (+ "via …" on the Elizabeth line), how many minutes away ("arriving" when it's a minute or less), the London arrival clock time, and where the train is now where TfL says so. The board refreshes itself every 30 seconds — the "Updated" time at the bottom tells you when it last fetched — and you can pull down to refresh or tap the refresh button in the top right. At Paddington, the Elizabeth line board shows only platforms A and B — the mainline station's platforms 11 and 12, which carry late-night trains ending at Paddington, are left out (v1.12). No sound in the first cut: unlike the TV, the phone doesn't chime when a train arrives. **Map** (new in v1.11) — tap "Map" in the List / Map switch at the top of the Boards tab and the whole Elizabeth line appears on Apple Maps, with a live dot for every train on the line: red heading east, blue heading west. Pinch and drag to look around. Tap a dot and the strip underneath tells you where that train is going, its next stop and how many minutes away, and the stretch it is on ("Between Farringdon and Liverpool Street"). Tap the same dot again to clear it, or tap another train. The dots are placed from TfL's arrival times — the Elizabeth line doesn't report where its trains are, so each dot is an estimate between stations, and the screen says so. The map refreshes every 30 seconds, and the "Updated" time tells you when it last fetched.

**Planned** — planned disruptions: strikes and engineering works. Switch between "Next 7 days" and "Trip · Dec 15–24". All dates and times on this tab are London time, so what you see is the day it happens in the UK (fixed in v1.4 — it used to show your phone's time zone, which put the Saturday strike under a Friday header). As of 16 Nov, the trip week shows the two RMT Tube driver strikes (noon Thu 17 Dec–11:59 Fri 18 Dec, and noon Sat 19 Dec–11:59 Sun 20 Dec) and a late-night Elizabeth line change on Tue 15 Dec. Strikes can be called off after talks, so check again closer to the date.

**Places** (was Stations) — **Favourites** at the top: your saved places (the hotel first, then ME London and any places you add, like Dishoom High Street Kensington), your favourite pubs, and your favourite stations. Tap one to set it as From or To, or to plan from there; tap the hotel to see its address and nearest station (Edgware Road, Bakerloo — a shorter walk than Paddington). **Add a place** is in the Favourites section: name it, give the address or postcode, tap "Find this place", and save it — the coordinates are stored, so journeys plan from the place itself, and the nearest station is recorded so it shows up in Recent and can be saved as a route. Below that is **Pubs** — the full list of 327, searchable, each with a star to favourite it. Then the searchable list of 97 stations (Zone 1–2, plus Heathrow, Stratford, Wembley Park, Richmond, Wimbledon and the other hubs you are likely to use), including Limehouse, Shadwell and Hampstead (added for the pub crawl). Tap a station to set it as From or To, or to star it as a favourite. At the top of this tab are the Settings: **App colour** — pick the app's accent from the Tube line colours (crimson included, for Roll Tide) — and a **Version** row, "TubeNavigator v1.12 (13)", visible the moment the tab opens, so you can tell at a glance which build is on the device you are holding. Below that are the Guides: the official Tube map PDF and the step-free Tube guide PDF, bundled in the app so they open offline, even underground. Below the Guides is **Emergency numbers** — tap 999 to call the emergency services, tap Text 61016 to text the British Transport Police about something non-emergency on a train or at a station, or tap +44 800 40 50 40 to call them from a US phone. (61016 is text-only and may not work from a US number.) At the very bottom is the same version as a footer — tap it for a Jeff station sign.

## TfL key (optional)

No key is needed. TfL allows about 50 requests a minute without one, which is plenty for one person. If you ever get rate-limited, register free at api-portal.tfl.gov.uk, copy the app_key, and paste it in Plan → TfL key (optional). It stays on your phone only and is never sent anywhere except to TfL.

## Tips

- Paddington is your base. Elizabeth line from Paddington gets you to Bond Street, Tottenham Court Road, Liverpool Street and Canary Wharf with no change.
- Christmas Day (Dec 25) there is no Tube at all — you are home Dec 24, so this does not bite, but Boxing Day service is reduced.
- If a journey looks odd at night, that is TfL routing you onto a bus because the Tube is closed.
- The weather strip says "Weather isn't available right now" only if WeatherKit cannot be reached (no signal, or a signing problem). On the phone it uses your real location; in the simulator it worked too, showing Paddington weather.
- The app icon is the TfL roundel with JEFF as the station name, and the launch screen shows the same roundel.

## Troubleshooting

- "Could not reach TfL" — you are offline or TfL is having a moment. Station search still works; try again when you have signal. (A name TfL cannot find at all now says so plainly — "TfL couldn't find that place" — instead of blaming the connection, and an ambiguous name shows a "Did you mean…?" list instead of erroring.)
- "Did you mean…?" — TfL found more than one place for the name you typed. Pick the right one and the journey re-plans from it. If the place you want is not in the list, type the full name or the postcode, use "Look up '…'" to have the phone find it, or save it as a place on the Places tab so it never has to be guessed.
- A line under a field says "not looked up — TfL will guess the place." — you picked the raw typed text instead of a suggestion. It will still plan, but TfL is guessing what you meant (that is how "me hotel" once planned from the wrong hotel). Pick a suggestion, or tap "Look up '…'", to get a confirmed place instead.
- No journeys returned — the Tube may be closed for the night, or the pair needs a bus. Check the Status tab.
- App will not install (Xcode error 3002) — unplug/replug the unlocked phone, remove any half-installed copy of TubeNavigator, Clean Build Folder, and run again. Read the exact error line in the dialog before guessing.
- Signing — pick your team in Xcode under Signing & Capabilities, as with HueRemote. Your paid Developer account covers this app.
